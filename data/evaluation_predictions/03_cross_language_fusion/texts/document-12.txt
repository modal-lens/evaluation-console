Preparation focuses on the conditions under which a comparison can be meaningful. The people reviewing the material check whether the inputs are sufficiently described, whether the same recording conventions have been used, and whether unusual circumstances have been documented. Records produced under different conditions are not treated as interchangeable simply because their summaries sound similar. The description explains which differences may matter and which questions remain open. This approach keeps the discussion connected to observable evidence rather than allowing an attractive explanation to determine which observations are retained.

The final handover preserves the relationship between the detailed record and its summary. A summary is useful for navigation, but it should lead readers back to the supporting discussion rather than replace that discussion. Questions about preparation, comparison, unusual observations, and interpretation remain visible in the complete account. The work is considered adequately documented when another reader can explain why the stated conclusions were reached and also identify where the evidence stops. This standard encourages careful communication while leaving room for reasonable uncertainty and for later evidence to refine an earlier interpretation.
