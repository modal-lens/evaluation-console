Responsibility for checking a statement is distinguished from the statement being checked. A review comment may request a clearer explanation without changing the established facts. A suggested improvement may identify useful future work without implying that the improvement has already been completed. The account keeps these categories separate so that recommendations are not later read as completed actions. It also preserves the reason for each revision. Another reader can then understand whether a sentence was changed to correct an error, remove an ambiguity, or make an existing limitation easier to recognize.

During the exchange of material, the receiving reader should be able to understand the subject, the evidence, and the unresolved questions from the text alone. Missing subjects, unexplained abbreviations, and compressed conclusions can make that task difficult. The review therefore favors complete explanations over short statements that depend on shared background knowledge. At the same time, length is not treated as evidence. An extended account still needs to show how each judgment relates to the material being discussed, and repeated claims cannot replace an identifiable basis for those claims.
