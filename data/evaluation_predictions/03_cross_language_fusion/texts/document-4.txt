Comparisons use a common basis wherever such a basis can reasonably be established. The account identifies the features that are held constant and the features that are allowed to vary. If a common basis cannot be justified, the observations are discussed separately rather than forced into a single ranking. This prevents a difference in preparation or recording practice from being interpreted as a difference in the subject itself. The purpose is to explain the evidence carefully, including the conditions under which an apparent similarity or difference would cease to be informative.

The review separates direct observations from interpretations. Direct observations describe actions and outcomes that can be checked against the available material. Interpretations explain possible reasons for those outcomes and identify assumptions that may affect the explanation. If several interpretations remain plausible, the account retains their respective supporting arguments instead of selecting the most convenient one without justification. A reader should therefore be able to distinguish what has been confirmed, what has only been suggested, and what would require additional evidence before a reliable judgment could be made.
